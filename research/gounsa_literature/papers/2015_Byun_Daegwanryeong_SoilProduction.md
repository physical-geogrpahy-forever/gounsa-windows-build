# 서지정보
Byun, J., Heimsath, A. M., Seong, Y. B., & Lee, S. Y. (2015). *Erosion of a high-altitude, low-relief area on the Korean Peninsula: Implications for its development processes and evolution*. Earth Surface Processes and Landforms, 40(13), 1730-1745. DOI: 10.1002/esp.3749.

# 이 논문을 찾은 이유
고운사 100년 soil/regolith production rate를 국내 산지 cosmogenic-nuclide 자료로 제약하기 위해 검토했다.

# 연구 유형
- cosmogenic nuclide geomorphology
- soil-production function
- catchment denudation and bedrock incision

# 적용 환경
- Daegwanryeong Plateau, Korean Peninsula
- high-altitude upland landscape
- alpine/frost-influenced soil production

# 핵심 결과
Reported regional rates include:
- bedrock incision: about 127 m Myr^-1
- catchment-wide denudation: about 93 m Myr^-1

The derived soil-production function shows weak soil-depth dependence, interpreted as potentially related to:
- highly weathered bedrock
- frequent frost action

A related Korean synthesis reports average Holocene soil production in the plateau of approximately:

```
0.05 mm yr^-1
```

which corresponds to roughly:

```
5 mm per 100 yr
```

as an order-of-magnitude soil-production benchmark.

# 고운사에 직접 사용할 수 있는 부분
This provides a Korean mountain/upland prior for total soil production.

It strongly suggests that over a 100-year horizon, background soil/regolith production is likely millimetre-scale rather than decimetre-scale unless local postfire/bedrock conditions greatly accelerate it.

# important caution
Daegwanryeong is:
- colder
- higher
- more frost affected
than Gounsa.

The weak depth dependence should not be transferred blindly.

# relation to model selection
The small 100-year magnitude argues for a reduced-complexity production model unless sensitivity analysis shows the weathering term materially changes results.

MErSiM can remain the mechanistic state architecture, but a Heimsath/Norton-style reduced production law may be sufficient for the first implementation.

# 최종 판정
**핵심 Korean soil-production magnitude constraint.**

Use to set sensitivity bounds and decide whether a complex regolith module is justified.
