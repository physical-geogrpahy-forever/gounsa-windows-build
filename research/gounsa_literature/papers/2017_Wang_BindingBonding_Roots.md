# 서지정보
Wang, B., & Zhang, G.-H. (2017). *Quantifying the Binding and Bonding Effects of Plant Roots on Soil Detachment by Overland Flow in 10 Typical Grasslands on the Loess Plateau*. Soil Science Society of America Journal, 81(6), 1567-1576. DOI: 10.2136/sssaj2017.07.0249.

# 이 논문을 찾은 이유
postfire dead roots가 살아있는 뿌리의 erosion protection 중 어떤 부분을 유지할 수 있는지, root physical binding과 live-root biological bonding을 분리한 직접 실험근거를 확보하기 위해 검토했다.

# 연구 유형
- concentrated-flow erosion experiment
- 10 grassland species
- root-mechanism decomposition

# 공간 구조
- 900 soil samples
- multiple imposed flow shear stresses

# 적용 환경
- Loess Plateau grasslands
- fibrous-root and tap-root architectures

# 핵심 과정
- soil detachment
- physical root binding
- biological/chemical root bonding
- rill erodibility correction

# 핵심 결과
Across all 10 species:
- plant roots reduced soil detachment by about 53.6%
- physical binding contribution: 41.4 percentage points
- bonding contribution: 12.2 percentage points

By architecture:
- tap-root systems: total 41.6%, binding 33.2%, bonding 8.4%
- fibrous systems: total 65.7%, binding 49.7%, bonding 16.0%

Fibrous roots were much more effective than tap roots.

The authors also derived WEPP rill-erodibility correction coefficients for the studied species.

# 고운사에 직접 사용할 수 있는 부분
Mechanistic state separation:

```
root physical network
 -> binding
 -> may persist temporarily after death

live rhizosphere/root activity
 -> bonding/exudate effect
 -> should decline rapidly with mortality
```

This makes it inappropriate to treat a dead root as either:
- fully identical to a live root, or
- immediately equivalent to no root.

# 새로운 coupling이 필요한 부분
The contribution percentages must **not** be directly converted into universal SWEHR exponent weights.

They are useful for:
- structural design
- sensitivity bounds
- interpreting live versus dead root protection

# 한계
- herbaceous species
- Loess Plateau
- not wildfire
- contribution fractions depend on root architecture and species

# 최종 판정
- **채택: physical binding vs live-root bonding mechanism**
- supports a two-component postfire root-resistance model
