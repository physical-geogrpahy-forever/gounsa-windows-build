# 서지정보
Ramirez, R. A., Jang, W., & Kwon, T. H. (2024). *Wildfire burn severity and post-wildfire time impact mechanical and hydraulic properties of forest soils*. Geoderma Regional, 39, e00856. DOI: 10.1016/j.geodrs.2024.e00856.

# 이 논문을 찾은 이유
고운사와 같은 한국 산불 산림에서 fire severity와 postfire time에 따라 root biomass, soil cohesion, hydraulic conductivity가 실제로 어떻게 변하는지 확인하기 위해 검토했다.

# 연구 유형
- post-wildfire field sampling
- laboratory hydro-mechanical soil testing

# 적용 환경
- March 2022 Uljin wildfire, South Korea
- moderate-low and moderate-high burn severity
- unburned comparison
- sampled 1, 4 and 6 months postfire

# 핵심 과정
- root deterioration
- root biomass loss
- soil cohesion
- saturated hydraulic conductivity
- ash clogging / hydrophobicity
- decayed-root macropore development

# 핵심 결과
- root deterioration depended strongly on burn severity
- burned plots consistently had lower root biomass than unburned plots
- soil cohesion declined with time after fire
- at six months, burned-soil cohesion intercept was about 1.80-2.30 times lower than unburned soil
- early postfire k_sat was lower because of ash/hydrophobic effects
- by six months, k_sat increased strongly as root deterioration created macropore pathways

# 고운사에 직접 사용할 수 있는 부분
This is one of the highest-priority local analogues.

It shows that roots affect **both**:
```
erosion resistance / soil strength
and
infiltration / runoff generation
```

through different time trajectories.

Therefore root mortality should not only modify `J`; postfire root/soil evolution can also influence hydrology.

# 새로운 coupling이 필요한 부분
The measured cohesion loss cannot be directly converted to SWEHR `J` without calibration.

The hydrologic effect of root-decay macropores also needs a separate infiltration parameterization if included.

# 한계
- six-month observation window
- targeted at hydro-mechanical slope properties rather than direct rill erosion
- Uljin geology/soil may differ from Gounsa
