# 서지정보
Sonoda, M., & Kurashige, Y. (2017). *Characteristics of surface soil creep on a forest slope in Japan*. Geomorphology, 288, 1-11. DOI: 10.1016/j.geomorph.2017.03.006.

# 이 논문을 찾은 이유
고운사와 기후, 산림, 풍화화강암이라는 점에서 비교적 가까운 동아시아 현장에서 wetting-drying와 강우가 실제 net soil creep를 만드는지 확인하기 위해 검토했다.

# 연구 유형
- six-year field monitoring
- strain-probe deformation measurement
- soil-moisture monitoring

# 공간 구조
- 33 m forest hillslope
- 12 monitoring sites
- upper/middle/lower slope comparison

# 적용 환경
- Central Japan
- forested slope
- weathered granite
- topsoil thickness locally up to about 1.3 m

# 핵심 과정
- soil drying
- soil wetting
- residual deformation
- cumulative creep
- rainfall-triggered miniature sliding

# 핵심 결과
Observed sequence:
```
drying
 -> downslope bending strain

wetting
 -> partial reverse deformation

incomplete recovery
 -> residual strain

repeated cycles
 -> annual net soil creep
```

On upper slopes, sufficiently large rainfall sometimes concentrated shear deformation and produced miniature sliding.

# 중요한 의미
This is direct evidence that temperate forest soil can have nonzero creep even without explicitly invoking:
- tree throw
- root-growth flux
- dry ravel

Wetting-drying and hydrologic deformation themselves create a residual downslope component.

# 고운사에 직접 사용할 수 있는 부분
Strong regional/process analogue for retaining a nonzero residual creep term.

The weathered-granite forest setting is substantially closer to a Korean mountain-forest application than alpine frost-creep or semiarid examples.

# 새로운 coupling이 필요한 부분
The paper does not provide a general numerical:
```
soil moisture change -> D_bg
```
equation.

Therefore using LPJ-GUESS daily soil moisture to dynamically calculate creep rate would require a new calibrated coupling.

# 한계
- one Japanese forest site
- strain/displacement monitoring rather than a general transport law
- miniature sliding should remain separate from background creep

# 최종 판정
**핵심 regional validation for nonzero wet-dry residual creep.**

Use to justify the process, not to transfer a universal coefficient.
